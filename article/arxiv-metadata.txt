Title: The turbulent boundary layer as an unconfined intermittent canopy: A structural synthesis

Authors: Guillem Borrell

Primary category: physics.flu-dyn

Comments: 17 pages, 13 figures. Perspective and synthesis article.

Abstract:
We develop a structural interpretation of the outer region of a zero-pressure-gradient turbulent boundary layer by combining three results that are usually discussed separately: the hierarchy of wall-attached momentum-transfer and vortex structures, the time-resolved renewal of those structures, and the geometry and dynamics of the turbulent/non-turbulent interface. The evidence supports an intermittent-canopy picture in which self-similar motions of many sizes overlap below the boundary-layer edge, while only the largest motions reach the intermittent region. Large objects do not form literal material trees growing continuously from the wall: they can form away from it and repeatedly merge and split. At the outer boundary, vorticity terminates near a threshold-dependent interface, whereas the irrotational strain outside the layer retains the potential-flow footprint of large internal motions. Strain-normalised stretching and strain-eigenvalue statistics also remain similar from the turbulent core towards the interface. These results are consistent with the interface being the convoluted upper support of the same turbulent field that populates the logarithmic and outer layers, but do not prove that each interface bulge is the tip of a particular attached cluster. We formulate the canopy as a constrained synthesis and propose joint object/interface measurements that would test the missing connection.
