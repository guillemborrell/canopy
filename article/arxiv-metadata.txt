Title: The turbulent boundary layer as an unconfined intermittent canopy: A structural synthesis

Authors: Guillem Borrell

Primary category: physics.flu-dyn

Comments: 15 pages, 10 figures. Perspective and synthesis article.

Abstract:
We develop a structural interpretation of the outer region of a zero-pressure-gradient turbulent boundary layer by combining three results that are usually discussed separately: the hierarchy of wall-attached momentum-transfer and vortex structures, the temporal similarity of those structures, and the geometry and dynamics of the turbulent/non-turbulent interface. The evidence supports an intermittent-canopy picture in which self-similar motions of many sizes overlap below the boundary-layer edge, while only the largest motions reach the intermittent region. Time-resolved tracking shows that tall attached structures preserve geometrical and temporal similarity: their dimensions scale with height and their lifetimes with height divided by friction velocity. They can therefore contribute to a canopy without introducing a new outer scale. At the outer boundary, we adopt the interface convention and conditional coordinate established by Borrell and Jiménez. Vorticity terminates there, whereas the irrotational strain outside the layer retains the potential-flow footprint of large internal motions. Strain-normalised stretching and strain-eigenvalue statistics also remain similar from the turbulent core towards the interface. We hypothesise that the interface is the convoluted outer envelope of the wall-attached cluster hierarchy: the intermittent region is where that hierarchy becomes sparse. The combined evidence establishes the structural and dynamical consistency of this identification and yields direct predictions for joint cluster/interface measurements.
