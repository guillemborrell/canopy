Title: The canopy hypothesis: the outer region of a turbulent boundary layer as an unconfined intermittent hierarchy

Authors: Guillem Borrell

Primary category: physics.flu-dyn

Comments: 15 pages, 9 figures. Perspective and synthesis article.

Abstract:
We propose the canopy hypothesis: the intermittent outer region of a zero-pressure-gradient turbulent boundary layer is the sparse outer reach of a wall-attached hierarchy, and the turbulent/non-turbulent interface is its convoluted envelope. Channel DNS shows self-similar vortex and momentum-transfer clusters associated with ejections, with length, velocity and time scales set by height and friction velocity. Independent boundary-layer studies recover analogous attached organisation to approximately 0.2 times the boundary-layer thickness. Interface-conditioned DNS further shows that wall-derived star scaling for vorticity and strain remains applicable through local excursions beyond the mean boundary-layer thickness, while normalised straining in the interface buffer remains core-like before turbulent-level amplitudes terminate sharply. Channel/boundary-layer comparisons also show that the competing global centre-channel mode is absent from the open boundary layer. Together these observations support a deliberately weak connection: the attached clusters are hypothesised to carry wall-similar physics to the local interface, including canopy peaks above the mean boundary-layer thickness. Joint cluster/interface labelling provides a direct test.
